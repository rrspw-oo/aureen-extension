How sleep shapes memory

8 min read

During deep sleep the hippocampus replays the day's experiences, transferring them to long-term storage in the cortex. Researchers have tracked this replay in both rodents and people.

In one study, participants who slept after learning recalled 20% more word pairs than those who stayed awake, even when total study time was the same.

The effect was strongest for material learned in the hour before sleep, which suggests timing matters as much as duration. Short naps helped too, as long as they stayed under 30 minutes.

None of this means you can skip studying. It means the night after studying is part of the studying.
